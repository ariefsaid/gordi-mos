The mobile cost of 5

5 tabs at 372px wide = ~74px each. Icons shrink to 20px, labels to 9px, gap tightens to 2px. "Money" and "Inbox" both 5-letter words competing for width. A high-schooler thumbing this at a roastery has to read. A's 3-tab bar breathes; C's does not.

Floor shares the owner's bar

The floor operator's "Now" is capture + next roast — but they carry the same 5-tab bar with Money in it. Money is invisible to them in content, yet present in their navigation. That's a destination the role never uses, occupying tap real estate it can't reclaim.

! Why this is the contrast, not the target

5 is the proliferation ceiling

Three destinations fit a high-schooler's working memory. Five is already past the comfort line — and the 6th is one stakeholder request away. C doesn't block the next append; it leaves the door ajar. This is the disease the redesign exists to cure, shown at its treated-but-not-cured state.

It works — that's the trap

Nothing here is broken. Money drills, Ops is a destination, Inbox triages. A stakeholder can point at it and say "this is fine." It is fine. It's also the exact shape that produced today's six-destination app: each destination justified in isolation, none dissolved. C is today with a fresh coat.

Mobile 5-tab is visibly cramped

On a 372px phone, 5 tabs squeeze icons to 20px and labels to 9px. "Money" and "Inbox" fight for width. The floor operator — whose job is capture, not navigation — carries a Money tab they will never open. The mobile cost of 5 is real and measurable in tap-target pixels.

Now is trying to be three surfaces

Because Money and Ops are separate destinations, "Now" mirrors a slice of each to stay useful as a cockpit. The result is a busy surface: money KPIs + ops strip + drifting tasks + objectives, all stacked. A's Orient composes the same data cleanly because Money and Ops aren't competing destinations.

It's here to justify A or B

C is the control. Without it, A and B look like arbitrary reductions. With it, the question becomes explicit: "do we relabel (C) or restructure (A/B)?" C makes the cost of relabel-only visible — so the decision to dissolve destinations is informed, not assumed.

The one thing C gets right

The Home/Dashboard merge into "Now" is correct and should survive into whichever variant ships. Two landing surfaces for the same "what needs me" job was always wrong. C fixes that one thing — and stops there.

The honest read

C is not a bad design — it's a safe one. It ships faster (less dissolution, less role-scoping machinery) and inherits today's mental model intact. If the redesign's mandate were "modernize without disrupting," C would be the answer. The mandate is "cure append-only nav growth." C trims; A and B restructure. Keep C in the room as the contrast that makes the case for fewer destinations visible — then ship A or B.
