✓ Why this spine wins

3 things to learn

A high-schooler can hold 3 destinations in their head. Home/Dashboard/Work/Operate/Plan/Inbox is 6 — past the limit. Each role lands somewhere they recognize.

Nowhere to re-append

The disease was append-only growth. With 3 destinations, the 6th tab is structurally blocked — the next surface must justify itself as a lens on Work or a panel on Orient. This is how Linear stays 3 destinations at 10× the scope.

Money stays heavy

The owner's Orient is the money cockpit. Finance doesn't lose a home — they gain a scoping that shows only their slice. The money job is served; it just stops being a separate destination.

Floor is one tap away

The capture FAB is on every screen, every role. A roastery operator's "Orient" is their next roast + a giant capture button. They never navigate to log.

Kitchen dissolves

No "Kitchen" in the rail as a destination. It's an Activity lens. Plan = a work item. Log = the global capture. Stock = a read view. Review = an approval queue under Work. The sprawl folds, not relabels.

The honest risk

Orient can become a dumping ground if role-scoping is lazy. This is a design discipline problem, not a structural one — and it's far cheaper to solve than "several apps".

Open questions for Arief
  1. Capture FAB vs No-FAB Rule. The floor job ("capture a roast in <60s") needs a persistent action. Is the DESIGN.md rule a relic of a manager-only assumption, or does it hold for a whole-company OS?
  2. Orient naming. "Orient" is a job verb. Alternatives if it reads too abstract: "Today", "Home", "Cockpit". The job is "orient me to what needs my attention" — pick the label that survives the high-schooler test.
  3. Activity vs Destination. Roastery/Kitchen show in the rail as Activities (not destinations). Does that hierarchy read, or should they be a second-level group under Work?
