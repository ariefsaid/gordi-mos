Current — why volume breaks it

At nine items the stream is merely flat. At 28 it stops being navigable, and the reason is structural: one column, one weight, one scroll. Switch to Busy day and the failures separate out.

Author collapse. Eight people’s signals sit in one list with the avatar as the only distinguisher, so “who is telling me this” costs a read per row. Project collapse. Tasks from five projects interleave with no grouping, so you cannot see that four of them are one project in trouble. No chase surface. Following up team members has no home at all — it is the third thing the brief names and the stream has no band for it. And the scroll hides the tail: the further down an item is, the less it exists.

Root cause underneath all four: the type ramp has almost no range. DESIGN.md documents 24 / 20 / 18 / 15 / 14 / 12 / 11px, and the app’s own --ds-font-size-* tokens sit 7–8% apart in the middle (xs→sm is 1.07, sm→md is 1.08). The widest adjacent step anywhere in the documented ramp is 1.20, and a legible hierarchy generally wants at least 1.25. If every size step is within a fifth of its neighbour, nothing can stand out — whatever the layout does. Every direction here works around that constraint with weight, colour, position and space. Fixing the ramp itself is the one change that would help all of them at once, and it is an owner decision, not a mockup decision.

A · Hierarchy — one thing commands the eye

Round 1. The single most consequential item is promoted: display face, size step, a tinted panel. Band labels shrink, meta lines dim, routine work drops back. The page gains one obvious entry point instead of nine equal ones.

Cost: it asserts a ranking the app must actually be able to compute. If “most consequential” is really just “first in the array”, this lies confidently, which is worse than flatness. Switch to Busy day: promoting one row out of 28 barely dents the wall — this direction was designed against nine items and does not scale.

B · Severity leads the line

Round 1. The reason moves from the right tail to the front of the row in a fixed 84px column, and rows regroup by urgency rather than by source. Severity becomes one left-aligned column you read straight down; the record’s origin drops to the meta line. No full-width eye sweep, and no colour-coded bars.

Cost: it needs one severity scale spanning signals, tasks and café logs. That is a product decision, not a CSS one. It also spends 84px of measure, and on phone the mark sits above the title. It holds up better at Busy volume than A, but it is still one long list.

C · Table — kind left, items right

Round 1. Each band becomes label-left / rows-right, so the kinds of problem form a scannable column and the rows tighten under them. Band labels move to the display face in sentence case, retiring the uppercase eyebrow. Routine work recedes.

Cost: the most conservative of all of them. It improves scanning without claiming any priority order, so it does the least about “what first”. It degrades to the current stacked layout on phone, meaning the floor persona sees no benefit at all.

D · Bolder — the count becomes the headline

Per bolder.md: amplify what the system already owns, make one decisive move, then quiet everything around it. The move is promoting the verdict from a whispered band label to display type at full strength — the same Plus Jakarta face the page already uses for titles, turned up. The greeting demotes to a meta line; it was never the point of the page.

Skeleton test: strip the copy and the structure still reads “one big number, then a short list, then a dimmed remainder”. The hierarchy carries the meaning, not the words.

Cost: it commits to a single headline number, so that number has to be the right one. And on a genuinely bad day a prominent red count is a stress amplifier, which is the opposite of “feels manageable”.

A finding, not an excuse. This reads quieter than “bolder” should, and the reason is in the design system: DESIGN.md’s type ramp stops at 24px (page-title) and owns no display step above it, so the verdict here is set at the ramp’s ceiling and amplified through weight, the red status token, position and space instead. bolder.md forbids inventing a primitive, so the honest options are to accept this ceiling or to add one display step to DESIGN.md for page-level verdicts. That is an owner decision and it is not made here.

E · Distill — one goal, everything else behind a count

Per distill.md: find the ONE goal and remove every obstacle to it. The goal is what do I do next. So this cuts what the row does not need to answer it: avatars, business unit, status pills, the second band, and the reason chip wherever the band label already said it. Five items show; the remaining 23 sit behind one honest line.

This is the most direct answer to “wall of text”, because it is the only direction that removes text rather than rearranging it.

Cost: it strips exactly the context the multi-person, multi-project problem needs. You regain calm and lose “whose” and “which project” at a glance — a real trade against the brief’s own words, not a free win.

F · Bento — the wall becomes tiles

Owner-requested. The vertical column breaks into tiles, and the three collapses in Current each get their own: signals by author, tasks by project, and waiting on people — the chase surface the brief asked for and the stream never had. Volume now reads as “six areas” instead of “28 rows”, which is the whole point: a bounded number of places to look does not grow when the work does.

Tile span encodes consequence — the lead tile takes four columns, minor ones take two. Deliberately not a uniform grid: craft-floor names same-size icon-plus-heading-plus-text cards as the lazy page scaffold, so every tile here carries real rows and is sized by how much it matters.

Cost: tiles fragment ranking. Within a tile you know what is worst; across six tiles you no longer have one ordered list, so “the single most urgent thing in the company” gets harder, not easier. On phone it degrades to stacked sections, which is Current with headings.

Corrected 2026-07-28 after owner review. Three real errors in the first cut, all fixed here. The grid did not line up: spans ran 4+2 / 3+2 / 2+2+2 against a six-column grid, so only the first row filled and the rest left holes — untidy, and not deliberate. Every row now sums to exactly six (4+2, 3+3, 3+3) and tiles stretch to equal height within their row. A region was named after its data: “Cafe 2 launch” was a tile heading, so the dashboard’s own structure would change name week to week and could never be learned. Regions are now stable product nouns — Needs you now · Waiting on people · Tasks · Signals · Café logs · My work — and the worst project appears as a caption inside Tasks. Invented mood labels are gone: “Worth a look” is “Signals”.

The principle that follows: in a tile layout, group by entity and sort by severity inside. Regions must be stable to be learnable; severity is volatile. Severity-first grouping (round 1, direction B) is right for a single list and wrong for regions.

I · Bento + signal feed — owner’s proposal

Owner’s question: “why not actually like a thread / twitter style in the right side and the bento occupies the middle part?” Endorsed. Signals are the only feed-shaped record on this page — chronological messages from many named people, read in order. Tasks, café logs and follow-ups are work items with owners and due dates. Putting them in one vertical stack was always forcing two different shapes through one layout.

So Signals leave the work grid entirely and take a standing column: avatar, author, message, severity chip, newest first, with the composer at the top. The grid keeps the work. This is the version that best answers the original brief — it removes the single largest contributor to the wall (12 signals from 8 people on a busy day) from the vertical stack, without hiding anything.

Costs, and they are real. A standing column spends ~300px of a 1180px measure permanently, so the work grid loses a quarter of its width even on days with two signals. Below roughly 1100px the feed has to drop underneath, which is exactly where the floor persona lives — so on phone this is Bento again, and the feed is a section at the bottom. Separation is by space, not a divider, because DESIGN.md’s Single-Border Rule forbids a second border colour to separate regions.

G · Delight — the earned moment is reaching the end

Per delight.md, Operate mode concentrates delight at first use, completion, recovery, or mastery, and reliability carries the rest. On a triage surface the moment that earns it is getting to the bottom of the queue — so the thesis is: Dewi should feel that the day is closable.

That produces two things, both useful rather than decorative: a truthful progress-through-the-queue line (“9 of 28 handled today”) so a long list reads as a diminishing one, and a real cleared state that names what was handled instead of an empty shrug. The failed check also gets its recovery named inline, because delight.md puts error recovery in scope and forbids jokes there.

Cost: progress framing invites gaming, and it implies every item is closable today when some are not. It also does nothing structural — pair it with F or E rather than shipping it alone.

H · Overdrive — drill in without leaving Home

You asked for the direction that matches the concern rather than the three I offered, so: the concern is volume, and the overdrive move is expanding a tile in place. Click any tile and it morphs to fill the region with its full list via the View Transitions API; Escape or Close morphs it back. Try it — the tiles are buttons.

This is the functional-UI kind of extraordinary the reference describes: the wow is in how it feels, not in an effect. 28 items become six tiles you can open and shut without a navigation, so there is no page load, no lost scroll position, and no back button to find your place with. Volume stops being a scroll problem and becomes a zoom problem.

Progressive enhancement: without View Transitions it is a plain show/hide and still works. prefers-reduced-motion collapses the animation to 1ms. Cost: the expanded state is a mode, and modes need an obvious exit — Escape and Close are both wired here, and on phone it must be full-screen with a real back affordance, which this mockup only approximates.
