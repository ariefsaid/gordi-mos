Home — four directions

Owner brief: “it’s just a wall of text… multiple signals from different people, tasks from different projects / process as well as trying to followup the team members. how best to structure this in way that even there’s lots to do, its still seems like a manageable.”

A, B, D, E, G and H are retired at owner request. All four survivors render from one shared dataset. Four owner-directed changes since the last round: the header is now one ~70px block (identity, state line and progress together, no separate strip); the state line is rule-driven, not written — use ↻ Message to rotate the wording and Day to walk from a fresh morning to a cleared queue; and the feed is capped at 6 with a real remainder. Layouts now respond to the frame’s actual width via container queries rather than the Desktop/Phone toggle, which is what was overflowing. Busy day is the real test: 30 items, 8 authors, 5 projects. Three owner-directed changes apply throughout: the progress strip is promoted to the top of every direction, avatars are gone, and the tile layouts are distilled rather than complete.

Current — why volume breaks it

At nine items the stream is merely flat. At 30 it stops being navigable. Author collapse: eight people’s signals sit in one list. Project collapse: tasks from five projects interleave, so you cannot see that four of them are one project in trouble. No chase surface: following up team members has no home at all. And the scroll hides the tail.

Kept unchanged as the baseline — no progress strip, avatars still present — so the other four have something honest to be compared against.

C · Table — kind left, items right

Each band becomes label-left / rows-right, so the kinds of problem form a scannable column and rows tighten under them. Band labels sit in the display face, retiring the uppercase eyebrow. Nothing is hidden: this is the complete list, just better organised.

Cost: the most conservative option. It improves scanning without claiming any priority order, so it does least about “what first”, and at Busy volume it is still a long scroll. On phone it collapses to stacked sections.

C+ · Table in tiles

Owner-requested hybrid. C’s label-left rhythm, laid out as tiles rather than one stack, and distilled — each region shows its top rows and a count for the remainder, so the page states the shape of the day rather than reciting it. Rows are tighter than F’s and carry no status pills.

This is the densest of the four: it fits the whole day above the fold on desktop at Busy volume. Cost: density is the point and also the risk — it is the least forgiving on a phone, and the 96px label column inside each tile costs measure that a narrow tile can’t always spare.

F · Bento

The vertical column breaks into tiles, sized by consequence rather than a uniform grid. Six stable regions — Needs you now · Waiting on people · Tasks · Signals · Café logs · My work — so volume reads as “six places to look”, and that count does not grow when the work does. The worst project appears as a caption inside Tasks.

Cost: tiles fragment ranking. Within a tile you know what is worst; across six tiles there is no single ordered list, so “the most urgent thing in the company” gets harder. On phone it degrades to stacked sections.

I · Bento + signal feed

Owner’s proposal, endorsed. Signals are the only feed-shaped record here — chronological messages from many named people — while tasks, logs and follow-ups are work items with owners and dates. One vertical stack was forcing two different shapes through one layout. Signals take a standing column; the grid keeps the work, distilled.

On a busy day this removes the single largest contributor to the wall — 12 signals from 8 people — from the vertical stack without hiding any of it. Cost: the column spends ~300px of a 1180px measure permanently, even on a two-signal day, and below roughly 1100px the feed drops underneath. So on phone, I is F.

J · Tabs + feed — one thing at a time

The least overwhelming of the set, and the most opinionated. Instead of showing every region at once in tiles, the work side shows exactly one, chosen by tab, with counts on the tabs so nothing is hidden — you always see that 7 tasks are late even while reading the café logs. Volume becomes a choice rather than a scroll. Signals keep the feed column.

Cost, and it is the real one: tabs hide state behind a click, so a problem in an unselected tab is out of sight — on a triage surface that is a genuine risk, and it is why the counts must stay on the tabs permanently. It also adds a mode to a landing page, and it is the only direction where the first thing you see is a decision rather than the work.
